About the founder

The people and purpose behind ItisScientific.

An independent Australian research enterprise connecting renewable energy, advanced chemical materials, scientific recording and intelligent systems through experimental evidence and practical engineering.

Research profile

Constantinos (Costa) Arvanitis

Founder and director of ItisScientific · BAppSc (Honours) · PhD candidate

From scientific research to practical systems

Costa's work brings together laboratory research, materials characterisation, renewable fuel concepts, sensing and the practical challenges of building independent research infrastructure. His doctoral research investigates how water and ionic-liquid treatments affect the spectroscopic and structural behaviour of poly(lactic acid) (PLA), using Raman and infrared spectroscopy among other analytical methods. Australian Society for Microbiology recognition · February 2015

Costa's public professional profile records recognition by the Australian Society for Microbiology as Top Undergraduate Microbiology Student in February 2015. This recognition belongs alongside his laboratory and academic research history.

Microbiology, academic recognition and yeast research

Costa worked with Professor Ian Macreadie at RMIT in honours microbiology research and published studies using yeast systems. His professional record includes a 2015 Australian Society for Microbiology Top Undergraduate Microbiology Student distinction. Earlier undergraduate and teaching experience included microbial isolation, antibiotic-producing organisms and yeast-fermentation practicals. The founder also reports work with preserved pure cultures and penicillin-related investigations; original laboratory notebooks are being checked before more specific claims are published. This research foundation informs ItisScientific's current yeast propagation programme and proposed cellulase and fungal enzyme research.

Azolla bioengineering and renewable fuels

The original 2019 doctoral research proposal examined Azolla and its cyanobacterial symbiont as possible sources of hydrogen, ethylene and ethanol. Its acetylene-to-ethylene nitrogenase link remains a biochemical research question, with original cultivation records. Read the Azolla programme →

RMIT university laboratory demonstrator · from 2012

Costa began working as an RMIT laboratory demonstrator while still an undergraduate student. His correspondence dated 6 March 2012 refers to his imminent start as a demonstrator while completing his science degree. He continued teaching laboratory practicals after graduating in December 2015. Contemporary records include a 2016 RMIT demonstrator payslip, Dr Gregory Nugent’s practical schedules and teaching manuals, and RMIT School of Science casual-payroll correspondence in 2017. The classes included cell structure and function, microscopy, osmosis, yeast fermentation, assessment of student practicals and related laboratory exercises. RMIT plant biotechnology and bioethanol research

From 2012, Costa volunteered in Dr Gregory Nugent's RMIT Plant Biotechnology Research Laboratory on ARC-supported biofuels research involving transgenic plants and recombinant cellulase expression. Their subsequent scientific discussions included plant growth regulators, biological assays, rooting gel and prospective doctoral and commercial research. CSIRO Clayton · spiroborate and antifungal chemistry · 2016–2018

Costa undertook research associated with CSIRO Manufacturing at Clayton under Peter Duggan, initially as an industrial trainee in 2016 and subsequently as a postgraduate student affiliate in 2018, with an interruption in 2017. His experimental work included synthesis and characterisation of spiroborates, followed by quantitative antifungal assays and collaborative discussions involving RMIT microbiology. Correspondence confirms three pure spiroborate products reported in May 2018; CSIRO records identify 30 November 2018 as the formal affiliate end date. See the original-record research history →

Automotive air-conditioning qualification and refrigerant-handling licence · 2019–2026

In 2019, Costa completed Certificate II in Automotive Air Conditioning Technology (AUR20218) at Chisholm Institute, Dandenong. A 7 May 2019 Chisholm email confirms that the certificate had been issued; a 5 July 2019 Victorian Government message independently congratulates him on completion. The Australian Refrigeration Council subsequently approved his AAC02 Automotive Air Conditioning Refrigerant Handling Licence in 2020, with renewals approved in 2022 and 2024.

ARC's 2024 approval restricted the authorisation to refrigerant-handling work on vehicle cabin air conditioning and prohibited installation, modification and retrofitting. The licence expired on 1 July 2026, according to ARC's expiry notice, and is not presented here as currently valid or as authorisation for food-service refrigeration systems. Commercial invention before the modern research enterprise

The founder's product-development record predates his present laboratory programmes. His 2015 correspondence records profitable sales of a Big M soft-serve ice-cream concept first exhibited at the Royal Melbourne Show in 1985, a proprietary Mirro Majik mirror paint associated with a historical provisional patent filing, and later soy-based soft-serve ice cream. The invention and product history distinguishes the founder's own products from the original Big M trademark and documents the sources and their limits.

A regional research base

The research site in Pinnaroo, South Australia, has been developed around analytical and chemical-processing equipment, a three-phase electrical supply and on-site solar and battery infrastructure. The aim is to evaluate chemical pathways against actual measurements, energy requirements, safety obligations and economic feasibility.

Renewable fuels

Research interests include ethanol production through fermentation, alternative carbon feedstocks and the scientific assessment of a possible calcium-carbide-to-ethanol route. These are development programmes, not claims of commercially demonstrated production.

Scientific collaboration with Professor Ian Macreadie

Research collaboration with Professor Ian Macreadie at RMIT contributed to the founder's two published peer-reviewed works, including the 2020 review of boron-containing antifungal mechanisms. Correspondence from March–April 2019 documents Professor Macreadie's involvement in the editorial proofs and final publication approval. Read the publication record →

Published scientific work

Costa is first author of a 2020 peer-reviewed review on boron-containing antifungal compounds and co-author of a 2016 article on amyloid-beta research using yeast models. The publications page links directly to the publisher and PubMed records, and separates these completed works from current research.

Research integrity

ItisScientific distinguishes established chemistry from experimental hypotheses, planned engineering from commissioned equipment, and regulatory permissions from broader claims of endorsement. Results will be described on the basis of documented measurements and reviewed evidence.
