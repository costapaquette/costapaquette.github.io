Current research · November 2026 · Renewable bioprocesses

From cellulase research to independent enzyme capability.

ItisScientific is developing a research programme for cellulases, related carbohydrate-converting enzymes and yeast starter cultures, bringing together years of RMIT biotechnology and microbiology experience to investigate future renewable ethanol feedstocks.

The proposed bioprocess research connects plant-derived cellulose, microbial enzymes, fermentable sugars and yeast conversion. The programme's laboratory equipment has reportedly been assembled; commissioning, analytical verification and production-scale performance remain outstanding.

Active research direction · laboratory commissioning and enzyme validation pending
The research basis

Scientific work across several connected disciplines.

01 / PLANT BIOTECHNOLOGY

Recombinant cellulases at RMIT

From 2012 onward, founder Costa Arvanitis undertook volunteer plant-biotechnology research with Dr Gregory Nugent at RMIT. In a 12 July 2017 email to the ATO, he described ongoing experimental work involving transgenic plants and the endogenous expression of cellulases for future ethanol applications. The record confirms a research direction, not an independently demonstrated commercial enzyme yield.

02 / FUNGAL MICROBIOLOGY

Aspergillus experience

In February 2021, Costa wrote to RMIT's Dr Kylie Boyce about planned work involving Aspergillus niger and ionic liquids in a PLA-degradation research context, stating that he had previously worked with the organism. Three related laboratory risk assessments were returned with Professor Mark Osborn's signatures in May 2021. This documents prior experience and research planning, not validated in-house cellulase manufacturing.

03 / INDUSTRIAL ENZYME RESEARCH

Cellulase and amylase evaluation

2017 correspondence with a cellulase supplier discusses enzyme activity, composition and potential laboratory evaluation for cellulose degradation. In October 2024, the founder separately enquired about amylases, suitable A. niger strains and cellulase-producing microorganisms. These were supply and feasibility discussions, not proof that enzyme production was established.

Research chronology

A long-term project, not a new claim.

  1. 2012 onward · RMIT plant research and undergraduate demonstrating

    Volunteer research in an ARC-supported plant biofuels programme associated with Dr Gregory Nugent, including recombinant cellulase expression. Contemporaneous March 2012 correspondence also confirms the founder began RMIT laboratory demonstrating while he was still an undergraduate; later work is supported by a 2016 payslip and 2017 casual-teaching records.

  2. 13 May 2014 · Microbial screening practical

    Correspondence records a soil-microbiology exercise assessing bacterial isolates, starch-degrading organisms, antibiotic producers, sequencing and organism identification.

  3. 18 May 2016 · Yeast fermentation demonstrator

    Dr Nugent sent the original fermentation practical manual to the founder as part of his university demonstrating role. The manual describes alcoholic fermentation, carbon dioxide production and responses to substrate and enzyme-inhibitor conditions, with reference to brewing and winemaking.

  4. 21 March and 12 July 2017 · Cellulase testing and applied plans

    A request to Dr Nugent for cellulase assays and an ATO email recording both recombinant-plant work and the intention to evaluate purchased cellulases.

  5. 2018 · Published-research collaboration

    Laboratory work with Professor Ian Macreadie on yeast responses and boron-containing antifungal research; original communications led to later publication.

  6. February–May 2021 · Aspergillus laboratory research planning

    Correspondence with Dr Kylie Boyce and three signed research risk assessments for planned work involving Aspergillus niger.

  7. 29 October 2024 · Enzyme and strain enquiries

    Enquiries about amylases, A. niger and cellulase-producing microorganisms for future independent production.

  8. November 2026 · Current R&D direction

    Commission the independent laboratory, establish traceable yeast stocks, assess the feasibility of microbial enzyme sourcing and measure enzyme performance before attempting broader biomass conversion applications.

Connected ethanol pathway

Two complementary biological supply chains.

INITIAL FEEDSTOCK

Purchased sugar + yeast

Develop an in-house bank of suitable yeast cultures and a reproducible propagation and fermentation capability. Ordinary sucrose fermentation does not itself establish a need for industrial cellulase additions, so this stage is assessed independently.

Yeast strain programme →
FUTURE FEEDSTOCK

Cellulosic biomass + enzymes

Explore enzyme-assisted conversion of cellulose-rich material into fermentable sugars. Effective biomass hydrolysis may need complementary enzyme activities and feedstock pretreatment; feasibility must be established by measured assays, economics and process evaluation.
