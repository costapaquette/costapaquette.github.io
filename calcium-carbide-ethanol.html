Chemical pathway · concept and feasibility

Investigating an alternative route to ethanol.

A historically grounded chemical route connecting calcium carbide, acetylene, acetaldehyde and ethanol, assessed as a possible renewable-electricity-enabled process.

Conceptual pathway — not a demonstrated ItisScientific production process

Stage 1: sugar fermentation while alternative chemistry is developed

The independent ItisScientific fuel programme now includes an initial planned fermentation stage using approximately 1,500 kilograms of purchased sugar and yeast microbiology. The founder's roughly 1,000-litre target is close to the ideal stoichiometric upper bound for pure sucrose, but actual recovered volumes must be measured and will be lower. This operating programme is distinct from the proposed acetylene-based pathway explored below.

From calcium carbide to ethanol: the chemistry

The research question is whether an alternative carbon-based supply chain could be competitive with fermentation or conventional petrochemical ethanol routes when electricity, carbon sourcing, materials recovery, engineering safety and life-cycle emissions are evaluated together. The reactions below are a high-level chemical map, not an operating procedure.

01 · CARBIDE FORMATION

Lime and carbon

CaO + 3C → CaC2 + CO

High-temperature, electricity-intensive conversion produces calcium carbide. Carbon monoxide and the upstream production of lime are important environmental and engineering considerations.

02 · ACETYLENE INTERMEDIATE

Carbide to acetylene

CaC2 + 2H2O → C2H2 + Ca(OH)2

Hydrolysis yields acetylene and calcium hydroxide. Acetylene presents substantial flammability and decomposition hazards; practical use requires specialist engineering and controls.

03 · OXYGENATED INTERMEDIATE

Acetylene to acetaldehyde

C2H2 + H2O → CH3CHO

Acetylene hydration can yield acetaldehyde. Historical industrial methods have used hazardous mercury-based chemistry; assessing safer alternatives is an essential research challenge.

04 · ETHANOL

Acetaldehyde to ethanol

CH3CHO + H2 → CH3CH2OH

Reduction of acetaldehyde completes the conceptual carbon pathway. Hydrogen supply, selectivity, energy input and purification would determine whether a real process is practical.

What is established, and what is not? The underlying reactions are well known. ItisScientific has not published validated integrated yields, pilot-plant performance or a comparative life-cycle assessment for this route. No commercial operating claim is made here.

The engineering questions

Electricity and heat

Carbide manufacture is energy intensive. The relevant metric is total electrical and thermal energy per litre of usable ethanol, not electricity price alone.

Carbon and life-cycle impact

Using solar electricity does not automatically make a route low-carbon. Feedstock origin, calcination emissions, carbon monoxide management, chemical recovery and transport must all be assessed.

Process safety

Acetylene, hydrogen, acetaldehyde, carbon monoxide and hot process equipment introduce multiple severe hazards. A formal hazard study and compliant engineered systems are prerequisites for any practical evaluation.

Comparative economics

The proposed route should be compared with fermentation of sugar or biomass and with conventional industrial routes, using realistic capital costs, consumables, recoveries, purity requirements and Australian compliance costs.
