Research archive · Original doctoral proposal · 2019 onward

Azolla, nitrogenase and renewable chemical production.

ItisScientific's research history includes a detailed proposal to investigate the aquatic fern Azolla and its nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterial symbiont as a potential biological platform for producing hydrogen, ethylene and ethanol. The research connects plant metabolism, microbial symbiosis, nitrogenase activity and renewable carbon feedstocks.

Documented research proposal and cultivation experiments — not a demonstrated production process

The original Azolla doctoral research direction

On 16 December 2019, founder Costa Arvanitis submitted a proposed doctoral research topic titled “Azolla as a New and Renewable Source of Hydrogen, Ethylene and Ethanol”, alongside a detailed RMIT confirmation-of-candidature proposal. The documents considered the physiology of the Azolla–cyanobacteria symbiosis and whether targeted metabolic regulation could influence the formation of industrially useful compounds.

The RMIT proposal discussed nitrogen assimilation, heterocyst development, nitrogenase activity and the possible influence of metabolites on the expression of nitrogen-fixing machinery. It also considered the possibility of exploiting plant biomass for conventional carbohydrate-to-ethanol production. These ideas were proposed for research and testing; the correspondence did not establish that the project received final school approval as a doctoral programme.

Foundational distinction: The nitrogenase-catalysed reduction of acetylene to ethylene is an established biochemical phenomenon and the basis of a well-known nitrogenase activity assay. It is not itself proof of a commercially useful ethylene-production route, nor does demonstrating the assay mean Azolla has been genetically engineered or that ethanol has been produced. The proposed innovation was to investigate regulation and useful exploitation of the biological system.

Four linked research questions

Research 01

Symbiotic nitrogen fixation

Investigate the nitrogenase-dependent metabolism of cyanobacteria associated with Azolla, including how nitrogen availability and heterocyst differentiation affect enzyme activity.

Research 02

Acetylene–ethylene biochemical link

Assess the established reduction of acetylene to ethylene by nitrogenase as a measurable indicator of activity, while exploring whether biological production could ever be made practically useful. No industrial conversion rates are claimed.

Research 03

Biohydrogen production

Examine the relationship between nitrogenase-linked metabolism, photosynthetic activity and biological hydrogen release, including questions of yield, metabolic constraints and sustained production.

Research 04

Plant biomass and bioethanol

Evaluate the cultivation of Azolla and duckweed for biomass, carbohydrates and cellulose-based renewable ethanol research. The separate ethylene-to-ethanol chemical concept remains a proposed downstream pathway.

Why the Azolla concept remains relevant

The founder also discussed the Azolla–cyanobacteria research direction in a March 2025 LinkedIn article titled Revisiting My Azolla Research: A New Perspective on Symbiotic Bacteria and Hydrogen Production. The public profile lists the article.

The proposal linked three disciplines: botanical biomass production, cyanobacterial physiology and downstream renewable-fuel chemistry. Its most unusual element was the proposal to investigate nitrogenase regulation in the Azolla symbiosis as a possible route toward biologically generated hydrogen or ethylene.

This original research direction is distinct from the company's subsequent plant-growth-regulator formulations, non-biological acetylene-to-ethanol investigations, and proposed propulsion materials. Together, they form complementary but scientifically separate parts of the broader ItisScientific portfolio.
